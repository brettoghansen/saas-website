The Problem

The Outreach Problem in 2026

Cold email is the default B2B outreach channel, and it's collapsing under its own weight. Inbox providers have made deliverability harder every year. The average business decision-maker receives dozens of cold emails per day and has become expert at ignoring them. Delete without reading, filter to spam, or unsubscribe — the reflexes are automatic.

LinkedIn InMail is oversaturated and expensive. LinkedIn's own data shows engagement declining as the feed becomes more promotional. The platform that once felt like a professional network now feels like a trade show floor — everyone selling, few buying. And LinkedIn's connection and messaging limits make true scale difficult without significant spend on Sales Navigator.

Paid ads work but require significant budget and don't scale the same way outreach does. A $5,000/month ad budget gets you a fixed number of impressions — outreach scales with effort and automation, not spend.

The result: most B2B teams are competing on the same three overcrowded channels with diminishing returns. Contact form outreach is different because it reaches businesses through a channel they control and monitor — their own website's contact form.

The Channel

What Contact Form Outreach Is and Why It's Different

When a business builds a website, they add a contact form because they want potential customers to reach them through it. That form connects directly to their business inbox — not their "promotions" folder, not spam. It's the same inbox their best clients use. It's the channel they check for new leads, partnership inquiries, and customer questions.

Contact form outreach uses that infrastructure for B2B prospecting. Your message arrives as a website inquiry, not as a cold email. The psychological framing is completely different: the business reads it as someone who took the time to find their website and fill out their form — which is exactly what happened. You're not one of 30 cold emails in an inbox. You're one of a handful of website inquiries that day.

This is the reason contact forms outperform cold email on read rates. It's not magic — it's framing. When a message arrives through a channel the business set up to receive inquiries, it gets treated like an inquiry. When it arrives as cold email, it gets treated as cold email. Same message, different channel, dramatically different outcome.

The key insight: Every business contact form is a designated channel for new business inquiries. Contact form outreach puts your message in that channel — and businesses treat those messages accordingly.
Under the Hood

The Mechanics: How Loylty Finds, Fills, and Submits Forms

Loylty's automation is built on Playwright — the same browser automation library used by enterprise QA teams at major tech companies. It interacts with business websites exactly as a human would, which means it works with JavaScript-rendered pages, dynamic form fields, and complex multi-step forms that simpler tools can't handle.

Here's what happens for each target in your campaign:

  1. Website visit. Loylty opens the business's website URL in a real browser context. It navigates to the contact page — finding it by scanning the navigation, footer, and common URL patterns.
  2. Form detection. The system locates the contact form and maps its fields. Name, email, phone number, company, subject line, message — each field is identified and prepared for submission.
  3. Message generation. In AI mode, Loylty reads the business's website content and generates a personalized message based on your template. The message references the business specifically — their industry, their services, their location — not a generic copy-paste block.
  4. Review queue entry. The prepared submission appears in your queue. You see the target business, the personalized message, and the mapped form fields. Nothing sends without your approval.
  5. Submission. Approved submissions are submitted by Loylty, filling in the form fields and clicking submit exactly as a human would. The business receives a standard website contact form notification from their own CMS.

The business never receives anything that looks like a bulk email blast. They receive a contact form notification from their own website — the same format as every other inquiry they get.

Volume and Quality

Scale: Volume, Pacing, and Quality

You can realistically send 50–500 contact form outreaches per day depending on your plan. But the key insight is that quality beats raw volume in contact form outreach, more so than almost any other channel.

A targeted campaign of 100 personalized messages to relevant businesses will dramatically outperform a spray-and-pray blast of 1,000 generic messages. Generic messages get ignored — or worse, they generate complaints that could limit your access. Personalized messages that demonstrate genuine relevance get read and get responses.

Loylty's daily limits exist for this reason. They're not an artificial constraint — they're a quality control mechanism. Most users start with 25–50 submissions per day while they dial in their messaging and targeting. At that volume, you can review every submission carefully, reject anything that looks off, and learn which message formats work for which industries. Then scale from there.

Best Use Cases

Industries Where Contact Form Outreach Works Best

Contact form outreach works across a wide range of industries, but certain types of businesses respond particularly well — and understanding why helps you target more effectively.

  • Local and regional service businesses — Contractors, HVAC, landscaping, dental, legal, medical. These businesses have real contact forms and actively check them for new job leads. Their forms are their primary digital inbound channel.
  • Home services — Roofing, fencing, plumbing, electrical, painting. High-ticket jobs, high motivation to respond to any credible inquiry, active websites with working contact forms.
  • Professional services — Law firms, accounting, financial advisory, insurance. Decision-makers are often the business owner or a small admin team who handles all website inquiries personally.
  • B2B services companies — Agencies, consultants, SaaS tools targeting SMBs. These businesses understand the B2B outreach game and respond professionally to relevant inquiries.
  • E-commerce brands with B2B components — Wholesale buyers, partnership inquiries, supplier relationships — all flow through contact forms.

The common thread: businesses that have built an online presence and treat their website as a legitimate channel for new business inquiries. These are the businesses for whom a contact form notification represents real opportunity — and they handle it accordingly.
