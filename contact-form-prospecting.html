The Two-Phase Problem

What Contact Form Prospecting Is

Most outreach tools split into two phases — a prospecting phase and an outreach phase. You build a list in one tool, export it as a CSV, reformat the columns to match what the next tool expects, import it, configure the campaign, fix whatever broke during the import, and finally reach out. By the time you're sending messages, you've spent half your time in data plumbing.

Loylty collapses both phases into one platform. Contact form prospecting means: you search for businesses you want to reach by niche, city, and state — they appear in Loylty's interface — and you add them directly to a campaign. From "I want to reach fence contractors in Dallas" to "campaign is running" in under 10 minutes.

The key difference from other tools: There is no handoff step. The same platform that helps you find prospects also executes the outreach. This eliminates the friction layer that makes most outreach programs feel laborious before they even start.

This also means your prospecting research is never stale. When you search the database and add businesses to a campaign, those businesses get outreached immediately — not after a multi-step export/import workflow that takes two days and loses a quarter of your records along the way.

The Workflow

Building a Prospect List: Search, Filter, Add

The search-to-campaign workflow in Loylty is designed to be as fast as possible. Here's how it works:

  1. Navigate to database search. Available from your Loylty dashboard under "Find Prospects."
  2. Enter your target niche. Type a business category — "fence contractor," "dental practice," "roofing company." Loylty handles variations and synonyms automatically.
  3. Set location filters. Choose a state, city, or both. Results are filtered to match.
  4. Review results. Loylty shows business names, website URLs, and form verification status. Businesses with confirmed contact forms are marked with a checkmark.
  5. Select businesses. Check individual businesses or select all that meet your criteria. Sort by verification status to prioritize confirmed forms first.
  6. Add to campaign. Click "Add to Campaign." Either create a new campaign inline or select an existing one. Done.

The entire process — from opening the search to having businesses in a campaign queue — takes under 10 minutes for a targeted list of 200–500 businesses. No spreadsheet. No import wizard. No column mapping.

Real Examples

Niche Prospecting Examples That Work

The best way to understand contact form prospecting is through concrete examples. Here's how different niches play out in practice.

Roofing Contractors

Search "roofing contractor" in states with high storm activity — Texas, Florida, Oklahoma, Missouri, Tennessee. These businesses get consistent storm-damage leads through their contact forms and take new inquiries seriously. A message from a supplier, subcontractor, software tool, or service provider offering genuine value lands well because they're already in the habit of responding to contact form inquiries. Dallas and Houston alone have hundreds of roofing contractors in the database.

Fence Companies

High concentration in suburban growth markets — Texas, Arizona, Florida, and Tennessee — where residential development is driving demand for fencing. Fence companies are predominantly owner-operated, which means the person checking the contact form submissions is also the decision maker. They respond to supplier inquiries, equipment vendors, estimating software, and marketing services. The contact form is often their primary new business inquiry channel.

HVAC Companies

Year-round prospecting opportunity with clear seasonal hooks. Summer in hot markets (Texas, Arizona, Florida) and winter in northern states (Minnesota, Michigan, Ohio) create obvious angles for outreach timing. HVAC companies range from two-person operations to 50-person businesses — the database has strong coverage across all sizes. They actively respond to supplier inquiries, software tools, and service partnerships.

Dental Practices

Contact forms on dental websites go directly to the office manager or front desk staff — people whose explicit job is to respond to inquiries. The contact form is the standard channel for new patient inquiries, so they're monitored continuously. A message from a dental supply company, practice management software, or marketing agency reaches the right person at exactly the right time. Response rates for dental practice outreach tend to be among the highest of any niche in the database.

Law Firms

Solo and small firm practices are actively seeking referrals, vendor relationships, and partnerships. Their contact forms are monitored by the attorney directly (in solo practices) or by a dedicated admin (in small firms). Immigration attorneys, estate planning attorneys, and family law practices are particularly responsive to relevant partner and vendor outreach because they're constantly evaluating services that improve client intake and case management.

Message Strategy

How to Write Prospecting Messages That Don't Sound Like Spam

The message matters more than the volume. Five hundred mediocre submissions produce fewer conversations than fifty great ones. These five rules apply to every contact form prospecting message, regardless of niche.

  1. Reference their specific industry — always. "I help fence contractors" beats "I help businesses." Specificity signals relevance. When the first line of your message tells the reader you know exactly who they are and what they do, the rest of the message gets read. Generic openers get skipped.
  2. Keep it short. Three to four sentences maximum. Respect their time. A local business owner reading their contact form submissions is scanning, not reading. A concise message with one clear point gets through. A long pitch gets abandoned halfway.
  3. Make a specific offer. "I'd like to send you our comparison of estimating software used by fence contractors in Texas" beats "I'd love to connect." The more specific and concrete your offer, the lower the activation energy required to respond. Generic asks produce generic (no) responses.
  4. Use your real identity. Name, email, company. No vague "our team" language. No hiding behind a brand. When a real person's name and email are in the submission, the business knows they're talking to a human being, not a bot. This matters more than any other element for local business response rates.
  5. Don't oversell in the first message. The goal of the first contact form submission is to get a reply — not to close a deal. A reply is a win. Save the full pitch for the email thread after they've responded. The first message earns the conversation; the conversation earns the sale.
On AI-personalized messages: Loylty's AI automatically follows these five rules by default. It reads the business's website before writing the message, which produces opening lines that reference what the business actually does rather than their category label. This is why AI-personalized mode consistently outperforms generic templates — specificity is built in.
